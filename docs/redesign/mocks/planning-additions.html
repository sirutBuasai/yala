Planning additions

Four candidates for the Planning page, each drawn beside what the page shows today. The figures are made up but shaped like a real ledger: $40k cash, $210k taxable, $180k pre-tax 401k, $45k Roth IRA, $20k HSA, $60k a year spent, $45k a year invested. The sliders in each section move only that section.

1 · Years of freedom, or an FI date

Years of freedom is net worth divided by the last twelve months' spending, against the years left until retirement. It assumes your money earns nothing, ignores tax and inflation, counts 401k money as if you could spend it today, and uses logged spending rather than your planned spending. The FI date answers the question people actually ask: when does the plan reach the FI number?

2 · Market risk

Today's projection compounds one fixed return every year. Real markets vary, and a bad run in the first years of retirement can empty a plan whose average return looked fine. The proposal runs 1,000 simulated markets around your expected return and draws where most of them land, with the share that last to your horizon age.

3 · Access before 59½

Retiring before 59½ means living on money you can reach without the 10% early-withdrawal penalty: cash, taxable accounts and Roth IRA contributions. This pane checks whether those cover the years until the 401k and IRAs open. Today the page has nothing like it.

Proposed pane
Access before 59½whether reachable money bridges early retirement

  • Reachable any time: cash, taxable accounts, Roth IRA contributions (not their growth), HSA for medical bills.
  • From 55: the 401k at the employer you leave in or after the year you turn 55 (Rule of 55).
  • Other ways in: equal yearly withdrawals from an IRA (72(t)), or converting to Roth five years ahead (Roth ladder).

4 · Tax on withdrawals

Today the FI number is spending divided by the withdrawal rate, as if every dollar withdrawn were yours to spend. Each account type is taxed differently when you take money out, so the plan needs to know each account's type, then gross up spending by the tax it will pay.

Proposed · each investment account's tax type

Set once per account in Manage, guessed from its name. A 401k with both Roth and pre-tax money is split into two accounts.

What to decide

  • 1 · FI dateRemove Years of freedom and put the FI date in its place? No new settings; one new KPI.
  • 2 · Market riskOne new setting (Return volatility). Monte Carlo around your expected return, as drawn. The other common method replays real market history from 1871 on, which keeps real crash sequences but needs a returns table shipped with the app.
  • 3 · Access before 59½A new pane, and one new setting (Roth IRA contributions). It needs each account's tax type from 4 to know what is reachable, so 3 depends on 4. With your retirement age at 60 it would simply say no bridge is needed.
  • 4 · Tax on withdrawalsA tax type on each investment account (Manage, stored in the ledger's account metadata) and three new settings. It moves the FI number, so Financial progress, Coast FI, the projection and Dashboard's headline all move with it. A flat effective rate, not tax brackets; brackets could come later.
