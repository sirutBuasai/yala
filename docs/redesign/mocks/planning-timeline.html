Planning timeline

Three ways to give the summary pane more to say, each drawn under the pane as it is today. Sample plan: born 1990, $455k invested, $55k a year spent, $50k a year invested, retiring at 60, planning to 90. Everything here is derived from figures the page already has; nothing needs a new setting.

Today

A · Phases and ages

The rail reads as three stretches of life: building to FI, working by choice until retirement, then drawing down. Ages tick along the bottom so each milestone reads as both a year and an age.

B · Balance along the way

The projected invested balance rides above the rail as a small area, so the timeline shows where the money peaks and how it runs down. Each milestone carries its balance, and a row of counts says how far off each one is.

C · What-ifs

The simulated markets answer what you would change: the earliest age you could retire and still succeed in 90% of markets, marked as a ghost on the rail, and a few nudges with their effect.

What to decide

  • Pick and mixA, B and C stack: phases on the rail, the balance above it, the ghost for the earliest safe retirement. Taking all three makes the pane taller; each alone keeps it close to today's height.
  • CostA and B read what the page already computes. C runs the simulated markets once per candidate age (about 40 runs of 1,000), which is still quick, but is the only one with a real price.
  • ClicksNone proposed: like the rest of the page, the timeline only reads. A what-if chip in C could set the assumption it names, if you want that.
