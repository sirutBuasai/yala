How far back the Year view reaches

The Year view draws every year from the first one you logged to the last, gaps included. After decades that is dozens of bars. Below are four ways to pick a time frame, each drawn with the current chart. Sample figures, not your ledger: 26 years, 2001 to 2026, with nothing logged in 2009.

Today: every year, labels thinned

No time frame. A gap year keeps its slot, drawn as an empty outline so the axis stays continuous. The bar chart's labels are thinned so they never overlap, which it does not do yet. This is the baseline the others are measured against.

A window you step through

The same span choices in a menu, plus arrows that slide the window a whole span at a time, the way the Month view's year stepper works. Any decade is two clicks away, and the header says exactly which years are showing.

An overview strip with a draggable window

Every year stays visible as a thin strip under the chart; the chart above shows the window you drag or resize on it. Best for seeing where a span sits in the whole record. It costs a strip of height on the board and is the fiddliest to use on a phone.

Decide

Which control

A is quickest, B reaches any decade, C shows context.

Suggested: B. It matches the Month view's stepper, and the header names the years on screen.

What the window covers

Only the year-axis charts (bars, Category by year, lines), or the whole board, so the KPI cards, Sankey and stat matrix read "2017–2026" instead of the lifetime.

Suggested: the whole board. A Sankey of fifty years beside a ten-year chart would answer a different question.

The default

All years, or the last ten.

Suggested: the last ten, which is every year you have today. The span lives in the URL and persists across pages like the other picks.

A picked year outside the window

Stepping the window away from a picked year could keep the pick or drop it.

Suggested: drop it, so the board never reads a year it no longer shows.
