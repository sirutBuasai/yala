The ledger now holds the real, verbose name; shortening happens at read time. Palette-first colours, 20-character soft target. Light and dark sit side by side so nothing hides in one theme.

Applied and verified. 13 accounts renamed to their verbose real names; institution: on all 26 balance-sheet accounts; bank_alias on 6 and account_alias on 5. bean-check is clean, and I ran your five-step rule in Python over the actual ledger — all 26 accounts land at 20 characters or under, so step 5 never has to fire. 1 · get_account_name()

Naming rules now settled, per your answers: cash accounts are named by institution alone; investments and credit cards are Institution + AccountName, with the account/card type spelled out. The add-investment form takes those as two fields — institution and account name — and the backend concatenates them into the leaf, so you decide the name and the code never infers it.

  1. Parse the leaf — de-CamelCase, lowercase interior particles, split letter→digit. BankOfAmericaCashRewards → “Bank of America Cash Rewards”.
  2. Under 20 characters? Done. 13 of 26 accounts stop here.
  3. Over 20, and both aliases exist? Apply bank_alias first, then re-check. “Bank of America Cash Rewards” → “BofA Cash Rewards” (17), which fits, so stop. Only if it were still over would account_alias also go in.
  4. No bank_alias? Apply account_alias. “Chase Ink Business Unlimited” → “Chase Ink Unlimited” (19).
  5. No alias at all? Fall back to the parsed leaf at full length.

The aliases are the two halves of the name, substituted independently — not a full-name override. The boundary between them is found by matching institution: as a prefix of the parsed name, which is why that key is now load-bearing rather than just a colour lookup. Every one of the 26 accounts currently lands at 20 characters or under, so step 5 never fires today — but a name with no alias would render at full length, so list rows must still ellipsize.

Steps 2–3 live in Python, not the frontend: networth.py already emits a label per account (currently the raw leaf), and format.ts separately de-CamelCases it. Putting the pipeline in the API makes data.json’s label the finished display name, and formatAccount() collapses to a passthrough — one implementation instead of two languages drifting.

6 · Decisions and open items

  • Declaring the institution also fixed five colours that guessing gets wrong. FidelityAmazon401k is held at Fidelity but named Amazon. The Amazon co-brands each contain two institution fragments — USBankPrimeBusiness and AmericanExpressAmazon — and USBank and Amazon are both six characters, so the longest-substring match in theme.ts resolved them by coin toss. BiltMastercard is issued by Wells Fargo but coloured as Bilt, and Income:Salary:Amazon is an employer that would match the Amazon institution outright. All of that goes away, and the wf / c1 abbreviation keys can be deleted from the guess map.
  • institution: is no longer optional. Splitting the alias into halves means the code has to know where the bank half ends, and the only non-guessing way to know is the metadata. It happens to be the same key the colour lookup wanted, so one addition settles both — I have written it onto all 26 balance-sheet accounts.
  • bank_alias repeats across an institution’s accounts, which can drift. “Amex” is now stated twice, on the Gold and the Amazon card; a third Amex card could arrive spelled “AmEx” and the two would disagree in one list. Cheap guard: when the form sees a known institution, pre-fill bank_alias from a sibling account rather than asking again. Worth doing before you add card number three, not now.
  • Step 3’s second half never fires today. Every account that overruns is rescued by bank_alias alone, so nothing currently needs both aliases at once. The branch is still worth having — “American Express Business Platinum” would need it — but it is untested by real data, so it wants a unit test rather than trust.
  • The cap stays advisory. Step 5 returns the parsed leaf at full length, so no list row may assume 20 characters even though all 26 accounts happen to fit today. The row lists here ellipsize; anything new must too.
  • Six Fidelity accounts still share one hue. Unchanged and unfixable by palette choice — it needs the chart-level fix (colour investments by tax role) that you deferred.
